Output tries to answer one question: **what measurable difference did a famous person's life make on Earth, both good and bad?** This page explains how a claim becomes a number, how numbers become scores, and why the rankings sometimes look surprising.

## 1. What we count

Every person has a positive side and a negative side. Both are counted in concrete units:

- **Lives**: lives saved or lost.
- **People**: people supported or affected in other ways, for example people fed by a technology.
- **Jobs**: jobs created, based on the headcount of organizations the person led.
- **Economy (USD)**: money, mostly fines, penalties and settlements found by official bodies.
- **Climate (tCO2e)** and **Nature**.
- **Culture**: major awards such as Grammys and Oscars, and Guinness records. Each award type is its own unit.

We don't count fame, follower counts, net worth or opinion.

## 2. The sourcing rules

A claim only counts if it passes all of these rules.

**Two independent, very trusted sources must agree.** Every counted claim needs at least two independent sources that agree on the number. A third source breaks a tie. "Very trusted" means peer-reviewed journals, governments and official bodies, national archives, major museums and memorials, university presses, and major newspapers of record. Blogs, fan sites, press releases and a subject's own marketing don't qualify.

**Official company filings are the one exception.** For a company's jobs or employee count, one official filing that the law requires, such as a US SEC Form 10-K or a filing with another regulator, is enough on its own. Filings are legally binding statements, so a second source adds little. Self-reported *impact* claims are different. A company's own lives-saved or safety figures never count on their own.

**If sources disagree, the lowest number wins.** If one trusted source says 1,200 and another documents 1,098, we count 1,098. Being conservative means a number on Output is a floor, not a ceiling.

**Harms need a court or official finding.** A negative claim counts only if a court, regulator or other official body made the finding. Allegations, lawsuits still in progress and news reports alone are not enough. A fine that was later cancelled on appeal doesn't count.

**Estimates are allowed, and they're labeled.** Some impacts can only be estimated, such as how many people a farming breakthrough fed. If two very trusted sources give an approximate figure, we count it and label it **est.** You can switch estimates off with the "Exclude estimates" link on any ranking.

**Unproven claims are shown but never scored.** If a claim fails any rule, we don't hide it. It appears on the person's page under **Not counted / unproven**, with its sources and the reason it failed, for example "does not confirm" or "conflicts". It has no effect on any total or score.

**Every entry is reviewed before it counts.** Anyone can submit a claim with sources using the [suggestion form](/feedback).

## 3. Who gets credit

The results of companies and foundations a person led or funded are credited to that person. If several named people share credit, the result is split by their stated shares, or evenly if no shares are stated. Each entry shows its credit type, for example *Direct*, *Organization*, or *Even split 1/2*. The counted amount is the claim's scale times the credit share.

## 4. Units are never added together

Each category is totalled in its own unit. We never turn lives into dollars, jobs into awards, or one currency into another category. The only conversion is between currencies within the Economy category, using official exchange rates. Each person's page shows a table of totals, one row per unit, with positive, negative and net values.

## 5. From totals to a score

To compare people across very different units, each measure is scored on its own, on a **log scale**, against the person with the largest amount on that measure:

> score = 100 × weight × log(1 + amount) / log(1 + largest amount anyone has on that measure)

The score is negative for harms. This has three effects:

- **Having 10 times more moves you up one step, not 10 times higher.** No single huge number can swamp everything else.
- **No unit can dominate.** Each measure is capped at ±100 × its weight.
- **Small fines barely register.** Economy is measured in millions of dollars.

**Weights:**
- Lives: **3×**, because life and death matter most.
- Economy: **0.25×**, because fines and settlements are penalties, not direct harm to people.
- Culture awards: share **0.5×**.
- Everything else: **1×**.

## 6. The Overall ranking

The **Overall** tab adds up each person's signed measure scores. Because harms are always negative, **a harm can only lower a score, never raise it.** To be ranked on Overall, a person needs counted entries on enough category tabs. People with too little data are listed under "Not enough data yet" and still appear on the tabs where they have data.

This is why the top of the Overall list holds a plant scientist and two Holocaust rescuers, not the richest or most famous people. Saving lives carries the most weight, and the evidence for those lives is strong.

## 7. Influence is shown, not scored

The **Influence** tab measures reach, not impact. *Languages* counts how many Wikipedia language editions have an article about the person, using Wikidata. This is the same measure MIT's Pantheon project uses. *Attention* is English Wikipedia page views over the last 12 months, from the Wikimedia Pageviews API. Influence is neutral. It is never positive or negative and is **not part of Overall**.

## 8. Limits we know about

- **Only measurable impact counts.** Art, ideas and kindness that can't be counted in our units are missing. A low score doesn't mean a person's life mattered less.
- **Using the lowest number underestimates.** Many people's real impact is probably larger than what we count.
- **Coverage is uneven.** Business leaders have legally required headcount filings. Many other kinds of impact have no comparable records.
- **Credit is a simplification.** Crediting an organization's headcount to its leader shows responsibility, not personal effort.

## 9. Corrections

If you think a number is wrong, use "Suggest a correction or a claim" on the person's page, or [contact us](/contact). Please include two trusted sources. We'll review it against the same rules.
