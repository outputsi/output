# Oskar Schindler: summary
*Source page: https://outputrank.com/people/oskar-schindler. Overall rank #2, score +101*

Oskar Schindler ranks #2 overall, behind Norman Borlaug. His score comes from one carefully documented number: **1,098 lives saved**. It is not an estimate. [E1]

**Where the number comes from.** On 18 April 1945 a prisoner list was drawn up at Brünnlitz, the factory camp where Schindler protected Jewish workers. It names **1,098 people, 801 men and 297 women**. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum (USHMM) Encyclopedia and the USHMM Holocaust Survivors and Victims Database ("Number of Persons (Exact): 1098") both record it. Yad Vashem and the New York Times' 1974 obituary give a higher figure, 1,200, so both agree that *at least* 1,098 were saved. Output always uses the lowest number its sources share, so 1,098 is the counted figure, with Direct credit. [E1]

**Why 1,098 lives gives a score of about 100.** Lives are weighted three times and scored on a log scale against the largest lives figure on the site, Borlaug's estimated 1 billion. On a log scale, saving about a thousand people is roughly a third of the way to a billion. With the 3× weight, that gives Schindler **Lives +101**, enough for second place overall.

**What isn't counted.**
- **"Rescued 1,200 persecuted Jews" (Yad Vashem):** marked unproven. USHMM doesn't confirm 1,200. It says "over 1,000" and cites the 1,098-name list. [U1]
- **107 survivors of a Goleszów transport** that Schindler and Emilie Schindler took in: marked unproven. The second source is a research website, not a very trusted source, and it only implies the 107 figure (120 people arrived and 13 had frozen to death). It also credits Emilie Schindler. [U2]

**Negative entries.** None are counted or pending.

**Reach.** Schindler has Wikipedia articles in 74 languages and had 1.03 million English Wikipedia page views from Oct 2025 to Sep 2026. This is not part of the score.
