# Norman Borlaug: summary
*Source page: https://outputrank.com/people/norman-borlaug. Overall rank #1, score +300 (est.)*

Norman Borlaug is ranked #1 on Output's Overall list, and the reason is a single, very large estimate. Output counts about **1 billion lives saved (est.)**, the largest lives figure on the site. Lives are weighted three times more than any other measure, so this one entry gives him the maximum possible Lives score of **+300**. [E1]

**What the 1 billion figure rests on.** Borlaug bred disease-resistant dwarf wheat, which became part of the Green Revolution. The Guardian's 2009 obituary credits that work with averting a famine that would have starved an estimated 1 billion people. US Public Law 109-395, the act that awarded him the Congressional Gold Medal, says his work increased "the number of lives Dr. Borlaug has saved to more than a billion people." Two independent, very trusted sources agree, so the figure counts. It is labeled **est.** because both sources give an approximate number, not a headcount. [E1]

**Mexico's wheat.** A second approved entry records that his new wheat varieties let Mexico become self-sufficient in wheat by 1956, confirmed by The Guardian and the World Food Prize Foundation. This entry adds **0** to his totals. It describes context rather than a counted number, and the second source doesn't restate the Guardian's 95% and 6× harvest figures. [E2]

**What isn't counted.** Two claims are shown as **unproven**:
- That he trained 2,000+ scientists from about 20 countries. CIMMYT's own account says "several thousand" from "more than 50 countries", so the sources don't confirm the same number. [U1]
- That his wheat improved living conditions for "hundreds of millions" (1970 Nobel speech). This is approximate and overlaps the 1 billion figure. [U2]

**Negative entries.** None are counted or pending.

**Reach.** Borlaug has Wikipedia articles in 77 languages and had 211,289 English Wikipedia page views from Oct 2025 to Sep 2026. Reach is shown for context and is not part of the score.

If you switch off estimates, his score drops sharply, because his ranking depends on the est. figure.
