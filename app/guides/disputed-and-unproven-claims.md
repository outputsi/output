Output leaves rejected and unfinished claims on the page. They sit under a heading, **Not counted / unproven**, with their sources. They do not change any total or any score. A counted benefit can sit directly above a harm the page refuses to score. This guide is about how to tell those rows apart, using the cases the pages already document.

The rules are summarized on [How we score](/methodology). A counted claim needs sources that agree on the number. A harm needs a court or official finding. If sources conflict, the claim does not get a guessed middle. The examples below are what that looks like on a person page.

## Haber: two death tolls, and no score

[Fritz Haber](/people/fritz-haber)'s Overall score is **+100**, from one counted entry. It is the fertilizer figure: about 1.2 billion people, est., People +100. There is no negative number in that score.

Under the unproven heading, the page lists a different claim. Haber proposed using chlorine gas, and a claim says the German chlorine attack at Ypres on 22 April 1915 killed 5,000 French and Algerian troops. Britannica is one source for 5,000. A Springer source on the same entry says the attack "reportedly injured about 5000 and killed about 1000." The page marks that pair **CONFLICTS**. The death toll is not agreed. Output also requires a court or official finding before a harm counts. This claim has neither an agreed number nor that finding. The page's own summary line is the one to remember: the chlorine-gas harm is disputed among sources (about 1,000 versus 5,000 deaths) and is not yet scored.

A larger fertilizer estimate, about 4 billion people, is unproven on the positive side for a different reason. The sources do not give Output a second confirming path to that higher number, so it stays off the score as well. Haber's +100 is only the counted 1.2 billion. The disputed harm is visible and worth zero in the math.

## Penalties that are on the page and still worth zero

Several of the largest dollar figures on the site are unproven. They are printed because hiding them would make the counted rows look like a full record. They are not in the Overall score.

[Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk)'s 2018 SEC settlement over the "funding secured" tweets is the clearest zero. He agreed to a **$20 million** civil penalty, without admitting or denying the allegations, and a court entered final judgment on 16 October 2018. The entry's scale is **0**. It is unproven. It does not reduce his Jobs +81 or his other counted scores. A related Fair Fund entry says $20 million from Musk and $20 million from Tesla, and that **$41.53 million** was later paid to **3,350** investors. That entry is unproven too. The page states both the $20 million penalties and the $41.53 million payout. It does not explain the difference, and neither figure is in the score.

The Benavides case on the same page is a harm a jury did find, and it is still unproven. A Florida jury found Tesla **33%** responsible for a 2019 crash that killed Naibel Benavides Leon and severely injured Dillon Angulo, two people. The claim states a **$242.57 million** judgment. Reuters' text on the entry says $243 million. The judgment was upheld in February 2026, and the page says an appeal is expected. Because the entry is unproven, those two people and that judgment are not scored. Musk's counted lives harm is a different row: the **14 deaths** in NHTSA's EA22002 review, which do count.

[Mark Zuckerberg](/people/mark-zuckerberg)'s page has the same split at a larger scale. Two European Commission fines are counted and pull his Overall score down to **+57**: **€797.72 million** in November 2024, and **€200 million** in April 2025. The page's economy total for those is about **$1.09 billion**, scored Economy −20. Separately, the unproven section lists Irish Data Protection Commission GDPR fines with scale **0**: €1.2 billion in 2023, and further fines of €405 million, €390 million, €265 million, €251 million, and €91 million. The DPC quote on the entry says the €1.2 billion decision is pending appeal, and that if an appeal is made the fine cannot be collected while the appeal is pending. A 2019 FTC press release on another unproven row announces a **$5 billion** civil penalty. Neither the Irish group nor the $5 billion is in the +57.

[Michael Dell](/people/michael-dell) and [Jensen Huang](/people/jensen-huang) are the shorter versions. Dell's counted score is Jobs +79 from about 97,000 employees. Two SEC penalties from 22 July 2010 are unproven: a $4 million personal penalty, and a $100 million penalty on Dell Inc. In both, the SEC release says the settling party did not admit or deny the allegations. Huang's counted score is Jobs +73 from about 42,000 employees. NVIDIA's 2022 SEC settlement, a $5.5 million penalty over crypto-mining disclosures, is unproven. The release says NVIDIA agreed without admitting or denying the findings. Those penalties are not scored.

## A dispute can still be scored

"Someone disagrees" is not the same as "unproven." The pages count some fines that the company says it will fight, when the page treats the decision as an official finding that has not been stayed or reversed.

On [Tim Cook](/people/tim-cook)'s page, the €500 million Digital Markets Act fine of 23 April 2025 is counted. The credit note says Apple said it would challenge the decision, and that a first-instance decision counts unless it is reversed or stayed. The €1,840,984,000 Commission fine of 4 March 2024 is counted as well. Together they are the page's $2.56 billion economy total, Economy −23. The challenge does not move them into the unproven section.

On [Sundar Pichai](/people/sundar-pichai)'s page, the €2.95 billion adtech fine of 5 September 2025 is counted even though an Alphabet filing on the entry says Google plans to appeal. The older Google Shopping fine, €2,424,495,000, is counted because the Court of Justice upheld it on 10 September 2024. The page's economy line for the two is about $6.08 billion.

Zuckerberg's counted €797.72 million fine is the mirror image of his unproven Irish fines. The BBC report on that entry says Meta rejected the Commission's findings and would appeal. The page still counts the fine. The credit note says EU appeals have no suspensive effect and that no stay or reversal was found. The Irish fines, where the DPC quote says a pending appeal stops collection, are the ones with scale 0.

[Larry Ellison](/people/larry-ellison)'s $3.014 billion HP judgment shows the other end of an appeal. The judgment was entered on 20 October 2016, the Court of Appeal affirmed it on 14 June 2021, and the Supreme Court docket on the entry says the petition was denied on 16 May 2022. It is counted. The headline rounds the dollars to $3.01 billion. The counted scale is $3.014 billion.

## How to read the two sections

On any person page, the **Counted entries** tables are the score. The **Not counted / unproven** tables are the record of what was submitted and did not pass. A conflict, a missing second source, a self-reported impact number, a penalty without an official finding, or a fine the page treats as stuck on appeal can all land in the second section. The label does not say which of those reasons applies unless the row itself says so. Haber's row says the sources conflict. Musk's SEC row says there was no admission. Zuckerberg's Irish row quotes the pending-appeal language. If the row does not give a reason, the page has not stated one, and this guide will not invent it.

The score at the top of the page has already ignored those rows. Haber's +100 does not contain Ypres. Musk's counted lives figure is 14, not the Benavides deaths. Zuckerberg's +57 does not contain the $5 billion or the Irish fines. If you want a number to affect a rank, it has to be in the counted table. The [methodology](/methodology) is the checklist. The person page is the evidence.
