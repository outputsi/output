A person page is a record and a score, stacked in that order. The score is a few lines near the top. The record is the tables under it, including the claims that did not count. If you read only the score, you will miss the sources. If you read only one row, you will miss which unit it belongs to. This guide walks the page from top to bottom. The example to keep open is [Warren Buffett](/people/warren-buffett), because his page has a counted benefit, a counted harm, and an unproven penalty. Other pages are here when they show a piece Buffett's page does not.

The rules those rows are applying are on [How we score](/methodology).

## The name, the photograph, and the two links

The name is the page's title. The photograph, when there is one, has a credit line and a license link under it. That credit is about the picture. It is not a source for any impact number.

Two links sit with the name. **Exclude estimates** rebuilds this page without est. entries. **Include estimates** brings them back. What that switch does to the whole site is covered in [estimates and hard records](/guides/estimates-and-hard-records). **Suggest a correction or a claim** goes to the feedback form with this page attached. A correction needs sources. The scoring page says two trusted ones.

## The opening sentence

The first sentence is the counted impact, in ordinary words, for the units this person actually has. Buffett's sentence is about jobs and about a dollar loss, because those are his counted rows. It does not mention the unproven OFAC penalty. Unproven rows are not part of "counted impact."

[Mary Barra](/people/mary-barra)'s sentence is only jobs: 156,000 jobs created. She has no counted harm, so the sentence does not mention the unproven $900 million forfeiture. [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk)'s sentence is longer, because he has several units, and it keeps them separate: people helped, people harmed, lives lost, jobs, and economic losses. The est. label appears in that sentence when the leading figure is an estimate. [Norman Borlaug](/people/norman-borlaug)'s does. Schindler's does not.

Under the sentence, the page points at the ranking where this person places highest and names other people on that list. Those names are navigation. They are not extra claims about Buffett.

## The category table

**Counted totals by category** is the page's honest ledger. One row per unit. Columns for positive, negative, and net. The line above the table says units are never added together.

On Buffett's page the jobs row is +392,400, from Berkshire's 2024 Form 10-K, and the economy row is −$896,000, the 2014 civil penalty. You will not find a third row that mixes them. On Cook's page you will find more than one economy row, because the two Apple fines are in different euro-years, EUR-2024 and EUR-2025, and a jobs row beside them. The euro rows are both money, so they can be combined into the single dollar loss in the opening sentence, about $2.56 billion. They are not combined with the 166,000 jobs. That distinction is the whole of [why units are not added](/guides/why-units-are-not-added).

If a row shows 0, the entry exists and adds nothing. Borlaug's Mexico wheat entry is that kind of row. It is counted, and its scale is 0.

## The Overall line, and the influence line under it

The Overall sentence gives the score and the components. Buffett's page shows an overall score and the pieces Jobs +88 and Economy −2. The score you see in the big type is the page's rounded score, **+87**. The components are already rounded to whole points. They are scores, not the raw 392,400 and not the raw $896,000.

Musk's page shows four components, Economy −14, Jobs +81, Lives −39, and People +54, and an overall **+81**. Borlaug's page shows one, Lives +300, which is also his overall **+300**. If a person has too little to rank, the line says so instead of inventing a score. The pages in the top of the Overall list do have a score.

Under that, a quieter paragraph gives Wikipedia languages and English page views for a stated period, and says this is reach, not impact, and is not counted in the score. Buffett's page gives 104 languages and 2.29 million page views from October 2025 to September 2026. Those figures never enter the +87. Ignoring that paragraph will not mislead you about the score. Treating it as part of the score will.

## Counted entries

**Counted entries** is the score, unpacked. The note above the tables says these are approved claims and that the counted amount is scale times credit share.

Each row has the claim, the sources, the scale, the credit, and the counted result. The sources are links, and many of them carry a short quotation so you can see the words the number came from. Buffett's jobs row links to Berkshire's 10-K and to the SEC copy, and the credit says Organization. His penalty row links to the coverage of the $896,000 judgment. If the credit says Even split 1/2, half the scale is what counts. Haber's 2.4 billion becomes 1.2 billion that way. If it says est., the row is an estimate and the Exclude estimates link will drop it.

Read the scale and the counted column together. They differ when credit is shared. They match when the person is credited with the whole amount. Barra's scale and counted column are both 156,000. The claim text also tells you that the filing's table, in thousands, shows Total Worldwide 155, and that 88,000 hourly and 68,000 salaried are the figures that add to the counted 156,000. The row is where that reconciliation lives. The opening sentence only has room for the result.

A negative counted row is a harm the page accepted. It will also appear in the category table's negative column. How a row earns that acceptance is covered in [what makes a harm count](/guides/what-makes-a-harm-count). Dimon's page is the place to see two large negative rows, $550 million and $920 million, both counted, next to a jobs row that is not reduced by them.

## Not counted, and why it is still on the page

**Not counted / unproven** is everything else: claims that were reviewed and did not pass. They have sources and a status. They do not have a credit share that feeds the score, because they do not feed the score.

Buffett's page lists a 2020 OFAC settlement of **$4,144,651** here. It is about sanctions violations by a subsidiary. It is visible, and it is not the −$896,000 in the economy row. The $896,000 is the counted penalty. The $4.14 million is the one that did not count. If you cite the larger number as his score, you are citing the wrong table.

The same section is where Haber's chlorine claim lives, with the conflict between about 1,000 and 5,000 deaths, and where Musk's $20 million SEC penalty lives with scale 0, and where Zuckerberg's Irish fines and the $5 billion FTC penalty live. A full reading of those rows is in [disputed and unproven claims](/guides/disputed-and-unproven-claims). The mechanical point, for reading any page, is that "on the page" and "in the score" are not synonyms. The heading tells you which is which.

## A short order of operations

Start with the opening sentence to see the counted units. Check the category table to see positive, negative, and net in each unit. Read the Overall line to see the signed scores and the total. Skip influence if you are asking about the score. Then read every counted row, including its quotations, until the number in the sentence has a source. Then read the unproven rows so you know what was left out. If an est. label is doing work you want to test, use Exclude estimates and read the page again.

That order works on a one-row page, such as [Lisa Su](/people/lisa-su)'s, which is 31,000 jobs and nothing unproven, and on a long page, such as Musk's. The [guides index](/guides) points at the ranking-sized versions of the same questions: jobs, lives, units, harms, and estimates. The person page is where each of those questions has to cash out as a row with a link.
