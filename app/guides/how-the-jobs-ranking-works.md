The Jobs ranking is a list of headcounts. The person at the top is not the one with the highest pay or the largest fortune. It is the leader credited with the largest counted workforce. Right now that is [Doug McMillon](/people/doug-mcmillon), at approximately **2.1 million** jobs, which the page scores as Jobs **+100**.

This guide is about how a filing becomes that number, what the credit line means, and why a much smaller company can still score in the 70s or 80s. The rules themselves are on [How we score](/methodology). The examples below are the entries already on the Jobs pages.

## What the ranking is counting

Open the [Jobs ranking](/?tab=jobs). The cards show a number of jobs, not a dollar figure and not a percentage. McMillon is first at +2,100,000 jobs. [Jeff Bezos](/people/jeff-bezos) is second at +1,298,000. [Warren Buffett](/people/warren-buffett) is third at +392,400. [Jamie Dimon](/people/jamie-dimon) is fourth at +318,512. [Satya Nadella](/people/satya-nadella) is fifth at +228,000. Further down, [Mary Barra](/people/mary-barra) is at +156,000, [Larry Ellison](/people/larry-ellison) at +141,000, [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk) at +125,665, [Michael Dell](/people/michael-dell) at +97,000, and [Jensen Huang](/people/jensen-huang) at +42,000.

Each of those numbers is the headcount of an organization the person led, on a date the source states. It is not a count of jobs that person personally hired, and it is not a salary bill. The unit on the page is **jobs**. Salary would be money. Net worth would be money. Output does not count net worth at all.

## Why one filing is enough

For a company's employee count, the scoring rules make one exception to the usual demand for two independent sources. An official filing the law requires is enough on its own. A US SEC Form 10-K is the usual example. A filing with another regulator can play the same role. The reason given on the scoring page is that these are legally binding statements. A company's own claim about lives saved is different. That kind of self-reported impact does not count on its own. Headcount in a required filing does.

You can see the pattern on the pages.

Walmart's Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended 31 January 2026 says the company serves customers through approximately 2.1 million associates around the world, about 1.6 million in the United States and about 0.5 million internationally. That is McMillon's counted entry. Credit type: Organization.

Microsoft's Form 10-K for fiscal 2025 says that as of 30 June 2025 the company employed approximately 228,000 people on a full-time basis, 125,000 in the United States and 103,000 internationally. That is Nadella's entry. A second SEC filing on the same entry says he was appointed chairman in June 2021 and chief executive officer in February 2014.

Amazon's Form 10-K for 2020 says that as of 31 December 2020 the company employed approximately 1,298,000 full-time and part-time employees. That is the counted jobs figure on Bezos's page. Fortune is also cited there for a later, higher count. The page counts the 1,298,000 from the filing.

Apple's Form 10-K says that as of 27 September 2025 the company had approximately 166,000 full-time equivalent employees. That is [Tim Cook](/people/tim-cook)'s jobs entry. The same entry quotes Apple's leadership page: Cook has been a director since 2011, was chief executive from 2011 to 2026, and serves as executive chair.

The exception is not only American. [Bernard Arnault](/people/bernard-arnault)'s page counts **211,552** employees from LVMH's 2025 Universal Registration Document, filed with France's market regulator, the AMF. The document says that excluding apprenticeships and vocational training, total headcount stood at 211,552, down from 215,637 a year earlier. The page counts the newer, lower figure. [Mukesh Ambani](/people/mukesh-ambani)'s page counts **38,314** employees from Reliance Industries' Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report for FY2025-26, filed under a SEBI listing rule. The report's own line is "Total employees (D + E) 38,314."

## The 156,000 on Mary Barra's page

[Mary Barra](/people/mary-barra)'s jobs entry is the one that shows how a filing can contain two wordings of the same workforce. General Motors' Form 10-K for 2025 says that at 31 December 2025 the company employed approximately **88,000** hourly employees and approximately **68,000** salaried employees. Those two figures add to **156,000**, and 156,000 is the counted scale. The same filing's table is in thousands and shows "Total Worldwide 155." The page states both. The counted figure is the sum of the hourly and salaried counts written out in the filing, not the rounded table total.

Her Overall score is **+82**, and the page says all of it is Jobs +82. The unproven $900 million ignition-switch forfeiture is on the page and is not in that score. More on fines that do not count is in [what makes a harm count](/guides/what-makes-a-harm-count).

## What "Organization" means

Every one of these entries is credited as Organization, or with a share of an organization. The scoring page says the results of a company a person led are credited to that person. The credit line is the page telling you that the headcount belongs to the company, and that the person is on the entry because they led it.

McMillon's page is specific about the dates. A 2013 Walmart filing said the board elected him to succeed Mike Duke as president and chief executive, effective 1 February 2014. A November 2025 announcement said John Furner would succeed him as chief executive from 1 February 2026, and that McMillon would retire on 31 January 2026. The 2.1 million figure is the workforce at the end of his last fiscal year as chief executive.

Ellison's page shows the other shape of the same credit. Oracle's Form 10-K for the year ended 31 May 2026 says the company employed approximately 141,000 full-time employees, about 49,000 in the United States and about 92,000 internationally. Those two parts add to the counted 141,000. The same filing says he has been executive chair and chief technology officer since September 2014, and that he was chief executive from June 1977, when he founded Oracle, until September 2014. The credit is still Organization. The page is not claiming he is the current chief executive.

The scoring page calls this a simplification. Crediting an organization's headcount to its leader shows responsibility, not personal effort. A Jobs score is not a claim about who worked hardest.

## Why 42,000 jobs can score +73

Jobs are scored on a log scale against the largest jobs figure. McMillon's 2.1 million is that largest figure, so he is +100. Everyone else is a fraction of that, and the fraction uses a logarithm, so the score falls slowly.

The pages show the result. Bezos, at 1,298,000 jobs, is Jobs **+97**. Buffett, at 392,400, is Jobs **+88**. Dimon, at 318,512, is Jobs **+87**. Nadella, at 228,000, is Jobs **+85**. Arnault, at 211,552, is Jobs **+84**. Cook, at 166,000, is Jobs **+83**. Barra, at 156,000, is Jobs **+82**. Musk, at Tesla's 125,665 employees on 31 December 2024, is Jobs **+81**. Dell, at about 97,000, is Jobs **+79**. Huang, at about 42,000 NVIDIA employees at the end of fiscal 2026, is Jobs **+73**. [Lisa Su](/people/lisa-su), at about 31,000 AMD employees on 27 December 2025, is Jobs **+71**.

Forty-two thousand is about one fiftieth of 2.1 million. The score is not one fiftieth of 100. It is +73. That is the "10 times more is one step, not 10 times the score" rule, visible in the actual list rather than in the formula alone. The scoring page states the rule. The Jobs pages are the worked examples.

## What is deliberately left out

A jobs entry does not include contractors, suppliers, or estimated indirect jobs unless a separate claim with sources has been counted. Nadella's page says the 228,000 figure is full-time employees as the filing reports them. Cook's page says full-time equivalent employees. Bezos's page says full-time and part-time employees, which is what that 10-K says. The ranking does not silently upgrade any of those definitions.

Pay is not on these cards. A fine is not subtracted from the jobs number. When Buffett's page also counts an $896,000 civil penalty, that penalty is an Economy row, scored at −2, not a reduction of 392,400 jobs. The jobs total and the dollar total sit in different rows. How those rows meet, and why they are not added as raw numbers, is the subject of [why units are not added](/guides/why-units-are-not-added).

The Jobs ranking also does not include someone merely because they are well known. If the page has no counted jobs entry, they are not on the list. Su's page, Huang's page, and Ambani's page each have one counted jobs entry and no counted harm. Their scores are only as complete as those filings. Anyone can send a sourced claim through the correction link on the page.

McMillon's +100 on jobs is also why he is third overall, not first. Lives are weighted three times. His jobs cap is +100. Borlaug's lives cap is +300. The Jobs list answers a different question from the Overall list: who is credited with the most counted jobs, on the filings the pages cite.
