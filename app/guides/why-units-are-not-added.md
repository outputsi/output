A person page never adds a life to a job, or a job to a fine, or a fine in euros to a Grammy. Each unit gets its own row. The Overall score adds the **scores** of those rows, after each one has been scaled, and it does not add the raw amounts. This guide shows what that looks like on pages that have more than one unit, and what would go wrong if the raw numbers were poured into one bucket.

The rule is stated on [How we score](/methodology). The pages below are the demonstration.

## Two rows on Buffett's page

[Warren Buffett](/people/warren-buffett) is sixth overall, and his page shows **+87**. That score is not 392,400 minus 896,000. Those quantities are not in the same unit, and subtracting them would be meaningless.

The jobs row is **+392,400** jobs. Berkshire Hathaway's 2024 Form 10-K says Berkshire and its operating subsidiaries employed approximately 392,400 people worldwide at the end of 2024. Credit is Organization. On the log scale against Walmart's 2.1 million, the page scores that Jobs **+88**.

The economy row is **−$896,000**. In 2014 Berkshire agreed to pay a civil penalty of $896,000 to settle a Justice Department and FTC case about a missed Hart-Scott-Rodino filing. A consent judgment made it official, so it counts as a harm. Economy is weighted **0.25×** and is measured in millions of dollars, which is why a sub-million-dollar penalty scores only **−2**.

The Overall line adds those signed scores: Jobs +88 and Economy −2. It does not add 392,400 and −896,000. The jobs stay in the jobs table. The dollars stay in the economy table. The only thing that crosses the boundary is the pair of scores, and the dollar score can only pull the total down.

That is also why Buffett ranks above people with far larger fines and below people with large lives scores. His jobs score is high. His fine barely moves it. [Norman Borlaug](/people/norman-borlaug)'s +300 never has to be converted into a number of Berkshire employees before the two men can be compared. Each is scored inside his own unit, then the scores are added.

## Four units on Musk's page

[Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk)'s opening line lists separate impacts, because the page refuses to collapse them. The default counted picture is about 78,000 people helped (est.), about 4 people harmed, 14 lives lost, 125,665 jobs, and about $136 million in economic losses from a €120 million fine on X.

Those are four units.

The jobs row is Tesla's Form 10-K figure, 125,665 employees at 31 December 2024, scored Jobs **+81**. The lives row is 14 deaths from NHTSA's EA22002 review, scored Lives **−39** while Borlaug's billion is the comparison point. The people row nets to **+77,996**: an even split of the roughly 150,000 daily Starlink users in Ukraine (75,000, est.), plus about 3,000 patients at a San Juan children's hospital (est.), minus 1 person in the Diaz harassment judgment and 3 people injured in the Cal/OSHA case. The economy row is the Commission's €120 million DSA fine on X, which the page shows as about $136 million and scores Economy **−14**.

The Overall line is Economy −14, Jobs +81, Lives −39, People +54, and the page's overall score is **+81**. You can check that the raw amounts were not smashed together. 125,665 jobs plus 14 deaths plus 77,996 people plus a euro fine is not a quantity the page ever prints. It prints four totals and then a sum of scores.

The weights are why those scores are such different sizes. Lives carry 3×. People and jobs carry 1×. Economy carries 0.25× and is measured in millions of dollars, so €120 million does not outweigh 125,665 jobs, and 14 deaths do not get thrown away either. The [Lives guide](/guides/how-the-lives-ranking-works) follows what happens to those 14 deaths when estimates are turned off and the comparison point shrinks.

## Money can be converted. Only inside money.

The one conversion the scoring page allows is between currencies **inside** the economy category, using official exchange rates. It is not a conversion of lives or jobs into dollars.

[Tim Cook](/people/tim-cook)'s page shows why the conversion exists. Apple was fined **€1,840,984,000** on 4 March 2024 and **€500 million** on 23 April 2025. Those are different euro-years, so they are different rows in the category table: EUR-2024 and EUR-2025. The page's single economy line, after the official rates, is about **$2.56 billion** in losses, scored Economy **−23**. His jobs row is separate: about **166,000** full-time equivalent employees, Jobs **+83**. The $2.56 billion is not subtracted from 166,000. The Overall score, **+60**, is −23 plus +83, the scores, not the raw figures.

[Sundar Pichai](/people/sundar-pichai)'s page does the same with **€2,424,495,000** and **€2.95 billion**. The category table keeps them on separate euro-year rows. The combined economy line is about **$6.08 billion**, Economy **−25**. The jobs row, **190,820** Alphabet employees, is Jobs **+84**. Overall **+58** is those two scores together. A reader who added 190,820 to 6.08 billion would be adding a headcount to a dollar figure. The page never does that.

[Jamie Dimon](/people/jamie-dimon)'s fines happen to already be in dollars, so no exchange rate is required. The $550 million criminal fine and the $920 million deferred-prosecution amount are the page's **−$1.47 billion**, Economy **−21**. The jobs row, **318,512**, is Jobs **+87**. Overall **+66** is −21 plus +87. Even when both numbers are large, they stay in different columns.

## A billion of one thing is not a billion of another

Haber and Borlaug are the cleanest pair. Haber is credited with about **1.2 billion people**, est., on the People measure, score **+100**. Borlaug is credited with about **1 billion lives**, est., on the Lives measure, score **+300**. The headlines are the same order of magnitude. The scores are not, because the units are not the same and the weights are not the same. People fed by fertilizer is not lives saved from famine. The site keeps those sentences apart on purpose.

Jobs show the same refusal at a smaller scale. McMillon's **2.1 million** jobs score +100, the jobs cap. That +100 does not get added to a lives total he does not have. Barra's **156,000** jobs score +82. Nobody on the Jobs ranking is listed in dollars. The [Jobs guide](/guides/how-the-jobs-ranking-works) is about that list alone.

Influence is a further unit the page shows and then sets aside. Languages and English Wikipedia page views appear on the person page under a line that says they are not counted in the score. They are not added to jobs or lives either. The scoring page says influence is neutral and is not part of Overall.

## What the Overall number actually adds

If you only remember one mechanical fact, remember this. The category table adds inside a unit. The Overall line adds across units only after each unit has been turned into a signed score, capped by its weight. A harm's score is negative, so it can only reduce the total. There is no step in which 1,098 lives, 2.1 million jobs, and a euro fine become one quantity of "impact stuff."

That is why the top of the Overall list can be a plant scientist, a man with a list of 1,098 names, and a retail chief executive, in that order, without anyone pretending that a life, a job, and a dollar are the same thing. The order is an order of scores. The rows above it are still in their own units, and the sources are still on the row.
