Some counted numbers on Output are documents: a filing, a prisoner list, a judgment. Others are estimates that two trusted sources were willing to put a figure on, without a roll of names. The estimate is still counted, by default, and it is labeled **est.** so you can see which kind of record you are looking at. A link on every ranking and every person page, **Exclude estimates**, drops the second kind out and rebuilds the scores.

This guide is about that label and that switch. The rule is on [How we score](/methodology). The before-and-after is the live Overall list.

## What the label is saying

An est. entry is an approved claim. It passed the source rules. The sources gave an approximate figure rather than an exact count, and the page counted the lowest figure they support. The label is not a slur and not a hidden demotion. Unless you turn estimates off, the entry is inside the totals and inside the score.

Borlaug's **1 billion lives** is est. The Guardian obituary and Public Law 109-395 both speak in estimates, "an estimated 1 billion" and "more than a billion." There is no list of names behind the billion. [Norman Borlaug](/people/norman-borlaug)'s page says so by the label, and his Overall score of **+300** includes it. He is first on the default Overall list because of it.

Winton's **669 lives** is est. The museum says "approximately 669." CNN states 669. The page also notes a Guardian line of at least 664. The counted entry stays at 669 and keeps the label. [Nicholas Winton](/people/nicholas-winton)'s Lives score of **+94** includes it.

Haber's **1.2 billion people** is est. Smil's "about 40%" of 6 billion, and Erisman's 44%, are approximate shares. The page takes 2.4 billion, splits it evenly with Carl Bosch, and labels Haber's 1.2 billion est. [Fritz Haber](/people/fritz-haber)'s People **+100** includes it.

Musk's two big positive people entries are est. About 150,000 people a day on Starlink in Ukraine, even split to 75,000, is est. About 3,000 patients at Hospital del Niño is est., and the BBC source on that entry does not restate the 3,000. Those two are why his page can say about 78,000 people helped (est.).

## What a hard record looks like next to one

Schindler's **1,098** is not est. The Brünnlitz list of 18 April 1945 names 1,098 people, 801 men and 297 women, and the museum database on the page calls the number exact. [Oskar Schindler](/people/oskar-schindler)'s Lives **+101** does not depend on the estimate switch. The 1,200 figure from Yad Vashem and the Times is a different, unproven row. It is not an estimate that was approved. It is a higher number the page declined to count.

Headcounts from filings are not est. either. McMillon's 2.1 million, Bezos's 1,298,000, Nadella's 228,000, Barra's 156,000, Tesla's 125,665, Dell's 97,000, Huang's 42,000, and Su's 31,000 are filing figures. They stay when estimates are excluded. So do counted fines and counted death tolls that are not labeled est.: Buffett's $896,000, Dimon's $550 million and $920 million, the Commission fines on Cook, Pichai, Zuckerberg, and X, Shipman's 215, and Musk's 14 NHTSA deaths.

A company's own safety slogan is not turned into an est. entry just because it contains a number. Tesla's claim that full self-driving could save more than 32,000 lives a year is on Musk's page as **unproven**, with scale 0. Reuters reporting on that entry says the company presented safety statistics that independent experts called misleading. The scoring page is explicit that self-reported impact does not count on its own. "Est." is not a back door for that kind of claim. An estimate still needs two very trusted sources on an approximate figure. A press line from the subject does not become est. by being approximate. It stays unproven.

## The switch, and the list it produces

The default Overall order is Borlaug +300, Schindler +101, McMillon +100, Haber +100, Winton +94. Three of those five scores depend on an est. entry. The **Exclude estimates** link on the Overall ranking rebuilds the list without those entries.

Schindler becomes first. His Lives score becomes **+300**, because 1,098 is then the largest counted lives figure on the site. The cap moved to the hard record. McMillon stays at Jobs +100. He never needed an estimate.

Winton is not on that Overall list. His only counted lives entry is est. Haber is not on it either. His 1.2 billion is est. Borlaug remains, because the Mexico wheat entry is still counted, but its scale is 0, so his Overall score on that view is **0**. He is no longer first. The billion was the rank.

[MrBeast](/people/mrbeast) is third on the estimates-off Overall list, at People **+100**. His counted entry is funding cataract surgeries for **1,000** people, and it is not labeled est. With Haber's billion-person estimate removed, that 1,000 is scored as the top of the remaining people measure. The default list does not put him there. The switch does. That is the label doing its job in public: you can see which ranks were standing on an estimate.

Musk's page shows the same switch inside one person. With estimates on, he is eleventh at **+81**, from Economy −14, Jobs +81, Lives −39, and People +54. The people score is mostly the Starlink and hospital estimates. With estimates off, those two entries leave. What remains on the people side are the counted harms that were never estimates, and the People component becomes **−23**. The 14 deaths are now compared with Schindler's 1,098 instead of Borlaug's billion, and the Lives component becomes **−116**. Jobs +81 and Economy −14 stay, because the headcount and the fine are not estimates. His Overall score on that view is about **−73**.

None of those underlying documents changed. The switch only chooses which approved rows enter the arithmetic.

## How to use the label when you read

If a card on the homepage says **estimate**, at least one counted entry behind it is est. Borlaug's card does. Schindler's does not. You can open the person page and see which row carries the label, in the credit column. You can follow Exclude estimates and see the row move into "Estimates (excluded)" rather than into the counted totals.

The label does not mean the sources are casual. Borlaug's billion cites a national newspaper's obituary and a statute. Haber's figure cites Smil and a *Nature Geoscience* paper. Winton's 669 cites the Holocaust museum. The label means the figure is approximate, and that you are allowed to set it aside and look at what is left.

What is left, on the lives list, is a list of 1,098 names at the top and a set of counted deaths below it, including Shipman's 215 and Musk's 14. What is left on the jobs list is almost the entire jobs list, because those numbers came from filings. The scoring page says estimates are allowed and labeled, and that using the lowest number underestimates. Both sentences are visible once you have used the switch once. More of the Borlaug case is in [why he ranks first](/guides/why-borlaug-ranks-first). More of the 1,098 is in [how the Lives ranking works](/guides/how-the-lives-ranking-works).
