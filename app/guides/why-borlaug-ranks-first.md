Norman Borlaug is first on Output's Overall list. His page shows a score of **+300**, and the Lives ranking shows about **1 billion lives** saved, labeled **est.** A wheat breeder outranks the people who run Walmart, Amazon, and Berkshire Hathaway. This page walks through the entries on his page so you can see why.

The full scoring rules are on [How we score](/methodology). This guide stays with one question: why this person, and why this number, sits at the top.

## The entry that does the work

[Norman Borlaug](/people/norman-borlaug)'s counted impact is one very large estimate. He bred disease-resistant dwarf wheat that became part of the Green Revolution. Two sources on the counted entry agree on the scale of that work.

The Guardian's 2009 obituary credits the wheat with averting a famine that would have starved an estimated 1 billion people. US Public Law 109-395, the act that awarded him the Congressional Gold Medal, says his work increased the number of lives saved to more than a billion people. Output's rule, when two trusted sources give an approximate figure, is to count the lowest documented figure and mark it **est.** The page counts **1 billion lives**, Direct credit, labeled est.

That single entry is the largest lives figure on the site. Lives are weighted **3×**, more than any other measure, because the scoring page treats life and death as the thing that matters most. The formula scores each measure against the person with the largest amount on it:

> score = 100 × weight × log(1 + amount) / log(1 + largest amount anyone has on that measure)

When you are the largest amount, the fraction is 1. Weight 3 then gives 100 × 3 = **+300**. That is the cap for lives. Borlaug is the cap, so his Overall score is +300. The Overall line on his page is Lives +300.

Nothing else on his page adds to that. A second counted entry says his wheat let Mexico become self-sufficient in wheat by 1956, confirmed by The Guardian and the World Food Prize Foundation. Its scale is **0**. It is context, not another billion. The page says the second source does not restate harvest multiples from the obituary, so those multiples are not a separate counted number.

## What the +300 is not

The score is not a prize for being famous, and it is not his net worth. [How we score](/methodology) says Output does not count fame, follower counts, net worth, or opinion. Borlaug's Wikipedia reach is on his page and is labeled as not part of the score. Influence, the languages and page-view measures, never enters Overall.

The +300 is also not a statement that a life saved in the Green Revolution has been converted into dollars. Lives stay in the lives unit. The economy measure is a different row, with a different weight. Borlaug's page does not have an economy total. His rank is the lives score alone.

Two claims on his page are shown under **Not counted / unproven** and do not move the +300. One is that he trained more than 2,000 scientists from about 20 countries. The page notes that CIMMYT's own account says "several thousand" from "more than 50 countries," so the sources do not confirm the same number. The other is a line from his 1970 Nobel speech about wheat improving living conditions for "hundreds of millions." The page treats that as approximate and as overlapping the billion already counted. Unproven claims are printed so you can see them. They are not in the score.

## Why the people under him do not catch up

The next Overall scores are much smaller, and the reason is the same formula.

[Oskar Schindler](/people/oskar-schindler) is second, with **+101**. His counted entry is **1,098 lives** saved. It is not an estimate. A prisoner list drawn up at Brünnlitz on 18 April 1945 names 1,098 people, 801 men and 297 women. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum records that list. Yad Vashem and a New York Times obituary give a higher figure, 1,200, so the page uses the lowest figure the sources share. On a log scale, 1,098 lives against Borlaug's 1 billion is far from zero and far from the cap. With the 3× weight, the page shows Lives +101. That is enough for second place, and it is not close to +300.

[Nicholas Winton](/people/nicholas-winton) is fifth, with **+94**, from **669 lives** saved, labeled est. He organized the Czech Kindertransport. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum says he brought approximately 669 children, mostly Jewish, from Czechoslovakia to Great Britain. The page also notes that The Guardian gives a slightly lower figure, at least 664, and that the credit there is shared. The counted entry stays at 669 and is marked est. because the museum's wording is approximate. Log scale again: 669 is close to 1,098, so the score is close to Schindler's, not close to Borlaug's.

[Fritz Haber](/people/fritz-haber) is fourth, with **+100**, and he is the useful contrast. His counted figure is also enormous: about **1.2 billion people**, est. Smil wrote that about 40% of the 6 billion people alive around 2000 depended on the Haber-Bosch synthesis of ammonia, which is about 2.4 billion. Erisman and co-authors, in *Nature Geoscience*, found that by 2000 nitrogen fertilizers were feeding 44% of the world's population. Output takes the lower figure, 2.4 billion, then splits the credit evenly with Carl Bosch, leaving Haber 1.2 billion. That entry is on the **People** measure, not Lives. People has weight 1×, and Haber holds the largest people figure, so he scores the people cap of +100. A billion people fed is not scored as a billion lives saved. That is why Haber can match a jobs cap of +100 and still sit far below Borlaug's +300.

[Doug McMillon](/people/doug-mcmillon) is third, with **+100**, all of it from jobs. Walmart's Form 10-K for the year ended 31 January 2026 says the company had approximately **2.1 million** associates. That is the largest jobs figure on the site, so his Jobs score is the jobs cap, +100. Jobs are weighted 1×. The cap is 100, not 300. A record headcount cannot outrank the largest lives entry, because the weights are different and the units are never added together.

The same pattern runs through the rest of the top of the list. [Jeff Bezos](/people/jeff-bezos) is seventh at **+86**, with a Jobs score of +97 from about 1,298,000 Amazon employees and an Economy score of −11 from counted fines. [Satya Nadella](/people/satya-nadella) is eighth at **+85**, from about 228,000 Microsoft jobs. Those are high jobs scores. They are not lives scores, so they do not approach +300.

## What happens if you turn estimates off

Borlaug's billion is labeled est. So is Winton's 669, and so is Haber's 1.2 billion. Every ranking has an **Exclude estimates** link. The live Overall list with estimates excluded does not look like the default list.

Schindler moves to first, and his Lives score becomes **+300**. He is then the person with the largest counted lives figure, 1,098, so he sits at the lives cap. Winton and Haber drop off that Overall list, because their counted entries are estimates. Borlaug does not disappear entirely. The Mexico wheat entry is still counted, and its scale is 0, so his Overall score on that view is **0**. He is no longer first.

That is the cleanest way to see what the default rank is made of. The +300 at the top of the ordinary Overall list is the estimated billion, weighted three times, against which every other lives figure is compared. Remove the estimate, and the hard record at the top of the lives list is the Brünnlitz list of 1,098 names.

[Harold Shipman](/people/harold-shipman) is a check on the same scale from the other direction. The Lives ranking shows **−215 lives**. The counted entry is that he killed 215 of his patients between 1975 and 1998, from the Shipman Inquiry's first report. His Overall line is Lives −78. A harm only lowers a score. It never raises one. Shipman is last on the default Overall list.

## What the rank is allowed to mean

Output's own limits, on the scoring page, apply here with particular force. A low score does not mean a life mattered less. Using the lowest number underestimates. The billion is an estimate from two trusted sources, not a census of named people. The page says so by marking it est.

The rank means something narrower. On the measures Output actually counts, with lives weighted three times, and with this estimate included, Borlaug's page has the highest signed score on the site. You can check the two sources from his page, turn estimates off, and watch the top of the list change. The [Lives ranking](/?tab=lives) and the [Overall ranking](/?tab=overall) are the two places that change shows up.
