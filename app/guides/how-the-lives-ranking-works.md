The Lives ranking counts deaths: lives a source says were saved, and lives a source says were lost. It does not count people fed, workers employed, or money paid. Those are other units. The person at the top of the default list is [Norman Borlaug](/people/norman-borlaug), at about **1 billion lives**, labeled **est.**, scored Lives **+300**. The person at the top of the list with estimates turned off is [Oskar Schindler](/people/oskar-schindler), at **1,098 lives**, and on that view his Lives score is also **+300**.

This guide follows those two entries, plus Winton, Shipman, and the 14 deaths on Musk's page. The weights and the formula are on [How we score](/methodology). The numbers below are the ones the pages already show.

## A list of names, and an estimate of a billion

Schindler's counted entry is a document with names on it. On 18 April 1945 a prisoner list was drawn up at Brünnlitz, the factory camp where he protected Jewish workers. It names **1,098 people, 801 men and 297 women**. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum's encyclopedia and its victims database both record that figure. The database line on the page says the number of persons is exact: 1098. Yad Vashem and the New York Times' 1974 obituary give **1,200**. Both of those higher figures still agree that at least 1,098 people were saved. The scoring rule is that the lowest shared figure is the one that counts. The page counts 1,098, with Direct credit. It is not labeled est.

The page also shows why 1,200 does not count a second time. A separate claim, "rescued 1,200 persecuted Jews," is under **Not counted / unproven**. The museum material cited there says "over 1,000" and points at the 1,098-name list. It does not confirm 1,200. Another unproven claim, 107 survivors of a Goleszów transport, stays off the score as well. The second source on that claim is not treated as a very trusted source, and the credit is shared with Emilie Schindler. None of that changes the 1,098.

Borlaug's entry is a different kind of record. There is no list of a billion names. The Guardian's 2009 obituary says his wheat is credited with averting a famine that would have starved an estimated 1 billion people. Public Law 109-395 says the number of lives saved rose to more than a billion. Two trusted sources, an approximate figure, so the page counts **1 billion** and labels it **est.** Direct credit. His page's second counted entry, Mexico self-sufficient in wheat by 1956, has scale 0. It does not add another life to the total.

[Nicholas Winton](/people/nicholas-winton) sits between those two styles. The counted entry is **669 lives**, est., Direct credit. He organized the Czech Kindertransport. Eight trains carried 669 children, most of them Jewish, from Czechoslovakia to Great Britain. CNN's obituary says his efforts saved 669 children, most of them Jewish. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum says approximately 669 children, mostly Jewish. The page flags a Guardian figure of at least 664 as a slight conflict, and notes that the Guardian's credit is shared. The counted entry remains 669, marked est. because the museum and others describe it as approximate. If you exclude estimates, this entry drops out. Winton is not on the Overall list once estimates are off.

## How 1,098 and 669 can both score in the 90s

Lives are weighted **3×**. Each lives total is scored against the largest lives figure on the site. With estimates included, that largest figure is Borlaug's billion, and his score is the cap, +300.

Schindler's 1,098 against that billion is Lives **+101** on his page. Winton's 669 is Lives **+94**. The scores are close to each other because a log scale cares about orders of magnitude. A few hundred lives and about a thousand lives are much nearer to each other than either is to a billion, and both are high on the scale once the 3× weight is applied. They are not close to +300. The gap from +101 to +300 is the gap from a named list of 1,098 people to an estimate of a billion.

You can watch the cap move. Exclude estimates, and Borlaug's billion leaves the Lives ranking. The largest remaining counted lives figure is Schindler's 1,098. The Overall list with estimates excluded puts him first, at Lives **+300**. The +300 was never a bonus glued to the word "billion." It is what the formula gives to whoever has the largest counted amount on that measure. Winton's 669 was an estimate, so he leaves. Borlaug's remaining Mexico entry has scale 0, so his Overall score on that view is 0.

## Negative lives are the same unit

A life lost is the same unit as a life saved, with the sign reversed. The Lives ranking is not only a list of rescuers.

[Harold Shipman](/people/harold-shipman)'s counted entry is that he killed **215** of his patients between 1975 and 1998, from the Shipman Inquiry's first report. The Lives card shows **−215 lives**. His Overall line is Lives **−78**. There is no positive lives entry on his page to offset that. A harm only lowers a score.

[Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk)'s lives entry is also negative, and it is not an estimate. NHTSA's investigation EA22002 found that 13 fatal Autopilot crashes led to **14 deaths** and 49 injuries, and that the Autopilot design was "not sufficient to maintain drivers' engagement." The counted figure is the 14 deaths. The 49 injuries are in the finding and are not a separate lives total. On the default Overall list, where Borlaug's billion is the comparison point, that entry is part of a Lives score of **−39**.

Turn estimates off and the comparison point changes. The same 14 deaths are then scored against Schindler's 1,098, the new largest lives figure. The Overall list with estimates excluded shows Musk's Lives component as **−116**. The deaths did not change. The scale they are measured against did. His jobs entry, 125,665, is not an estimate, so the Jobs **+81** stays. The €120 million fine on X stays. The large positive people entries on his page are estimates, so the People score on that view goes to **−23**, from the counted harms that are not estimates. His Overall score on that view is about **−73**.

## Lives are not "people"

[Fritz Haber](/people/fritz-haber) is easy to misread as a lives entry. He is not on the Lives ranking. His +100 is the People measure: about 1.2 billion people who depend on nitrogen fertilizer, est., after an even split with Carl Bosch. The scoring page keeps "lives saved or lost" separate from "people supported or affected in other ways, for example people fed by a technology." Haber's page follows that split. The chlorine-gas claim on his page is a lives-shaped harm, and it is **not** on the Lives ranking, because the sources conflict and it is unproven. That case is covered in [disputed and unproven claims](/guides/disputed-and-unproven-claims).

The practical test is the unit column on the person's page. If it says lives, it is on this ranking. If it says people, jobs, or a currency, it is not, however large the number is. Borlaug's billion and Schindler's 1,098 share a unit. Haber's 1.2 billion does not share it with them. That is why a plant scientist and a man with a list of 1,098 names occupy the top of the default Overall list, and why the order changes when the estimate is switched off. The [Lives ranking](/?tab=lives) is the place to see the unit on its own.
