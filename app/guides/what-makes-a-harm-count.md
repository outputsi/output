A negative entry counts only when a court, a regulator, or another official body has made the finding. An allegation, a lawsuit still underway, a news report by itself, or a fine later undone on appeal is not enough. When a harm does count, its score is negative, and it can only lower someone's place. It cannot raise it.

This guide is a tour of harms the pages count, and harms they show but do not score. The rule is on [How we score](/methodology). The cases are the rows already published.

## Official findings that count

The simplest counted harm on the site is a small one, which is useful because the size is not what qualifies it. [Warren Buffett](/people/warren-buffett)'s page counts a **$896,000** civil penalty from 2014. Berkshire Hathaway agreed to pay it to settle a Justice Department and FTC case about a missed premerger filing. The case ended in a consent final judgment. Reuters, quoted on the entry, reports Buffett saying they made a mistake when they overlooked the filing. A consent judgment is an official finding. The penalty counts. Because economy is weighted 0.25× and measured in millions of dollars, the page scores it Economy **−2**. The jobs score of +88 falls to an overall **+87**. The fine did not need to be large to qualify. It needed a finding.

[Jamie Dimon](/people/jamie-dimon)'s page counts two criminal outcomes, both already in dollars. On 20 May 2015 JPMorgan's parent company agreed to a guilty plea and a **$550 million** fine for an FX spot-market price-fixing conspiracy. The Justice Department release says the conduct ran from at least July 2010 until January 2013. On 29 September 2020 the same department announced a deferred prosecution agreement under which JPMorgan would pay **over $920 million** for precious-metals and Treasuries spoofing. The page counts **$920 million**. The two amounts are the page's **−$1.47 billion**, scored Economy **−21**. A guilty plea is a finding. The deferred prosecution agreement on this entry is counted as well, with the department's releases as the sources. His jobs score of +87 is pulled down to an overall **+66**. The jobs number itself is not reduced.

[Larry Ellison](/people/larry-ellison)'s counted harm is a damages judgment, not a regulator's fine. On 20 October 2016 a California court entered judgment against Oracle for **$3.014 billion** in the HP Itanium contract case. The Court of Appeal affirmed on 14 June 2021. The entry quotes the court: "we affirm the judgment." The Supreme Court docket linked on the page says the petition was denied on 16 May 2022. The page's headline rounds the figure to $3.01 billion. The counted scale is $3.014 billion, Economy **−23**. That is what a finished appeal looks like when the harm still counts.

Lives can be the harm, with no money in the row at all. [Harold Shipman](/people/harold-shipman)'s page counts **215** patients killed between 1975 and 1998, from the Shipman Inquiry's first report. The Lives ranking shows −215 lives. His Overall component is Lives **−78**. [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk)'s page counts **14** deaths from NHTSA investigation EA22002, a review that also described 49 injuries and found the Autopilot design insufficient to keep drivers engaged. Those 14 are a counted lives harm. The Cal/OSHA entry counts **3** people seriously injured in a 2013 molten-aluminum release, with $89,000 in fines stated in the claim. The counted unit is the 3 people, not the dollars. The Diaz entry counts **1** person: a jury found Tesla liable for racial harassment, and a 2023 retrial award of about $3.2 million was upheld that October.

The Commission's **€120 million** fine on X, issued 5 December 2025 under the Digital Services Act, is Musk's counted economy harm. The page says no stay or reversal was found, and it credits him as controlling owner. The page shows the dollars as about **$136 million**, Economy **−14**.

## First instance can count while an appeal is talked about

The scoring page says a first-instance ruling counts unless it is reversed or stayed. Several pages apply that in the credit note, in plain sight.

Cook's **€500 million** Digital Markets Act fine of 23 April 2025 is counted. The note says Apple said it would challenge it, and that a first-instance decision counts unless reversed or stayed. It is in the $2.56 billion economy total. It is not in the unproven section.

Pichai's **€2.95 billion** fine of 5 September 2025 is counted. An Alphabet filing on the entry says Google plans to appeal. The note says a first-instance decision counts unless reversed or stayed. The older **€2,424,495,000** Shopping fine is counted because the Court of Justice dismissed the appeal and upheld the judgment on 10 September 2024. Both are inside the page's $6.08 billion economy line.

Zuckerberg's **€797.72 million** Marketplace fine of 14 November 2024 is counted even though the BBC report on the entry says Meta rejected the findings and would appeal. The note says EU appeals have no suspensive effect and that no stay or reversal was found. The **€200 million** "consent or pay" fine of 23 April 2025 is counted on the same basis. Those two are the $1.09 billion economy total, Economy −20, which takes his overall score to **+57**. The BBC's "€798 million" is the same fine described in rounder words. The counted scale is the Commission's €797.72 million.

## What stays in the unproven section

The mirror image is a penalty the page will not score.

Haber's chlorine claim is the disputed death toll. One source says 5,000 dead at Ypres on 22 April 1915. Another says about 1,000 dead and about 5,000 injured. The page marks the conflict. There is no agreed number and no court finding attached to a counted row. The harm is not scored. His +100 does not contain it. The details are in [disputed and unproven claims](/guides/disputed-and-unproven-claims).

Musk's **$20 million** SEC penalty is unproven and its scale is 0, even though a court entered judgment, because the entry is the settlement in which he did not admit or deny the allegations. The Benavides judgment, $242.57 million on the claim, is unproven while the page says an appeal is expected. Those are not in his counted lives total of 14.

Zuckerberg's Irish GDPR fines are one unproven entry with scale 0. The list on the row is €1.2 billion in 2023 and further fines of €405 million, €390 million, €265 million, €251 million, and €91 million. The DPC quote says the €1.2 billion decision is pending appeal and that the fine cannot be collected during the appeal. The separate **$5 billion** FTC penalty from 2019 is its own unproven row. Neither is inside the +57.

Dell's $4 million personal SEC penalty and Dell Inc.'s $100 million penalty, both dated 22 July 2010, are unproven. The releases say the parties settled without admitting or denying the allegations. Huang's $5.5 million NVIDIA penalty from 2022 is unproven on the same wording. Their jobs scores, +79 and +73, do not include those dollars.

Barra's page lists a **$900 million** forfeiture under a 2015 deferred prosecution agreement over the ignition switch, with Justice Department sources, and marks it unproven. It has no effect on her Jobs +82. The page does not count it as an economy harm.

## Why a huge fine can move the score less than a short list of deaths

Economy's weight is a quarter of the ordinary weight, and the unit inside that score is millions of dollars. The scoring page says small fines barely register, because a fine is a penalty, not itself a count of people harmed. Buffett's $896,000 is the illustration: it qualifies, and it costs 2 points. Dimon's $1.47 billion costs 21 points. Pichai's $6.08 billion costs 25 points. Ellison's $3.01 billion headline figure costs 23 points. None of those economy scores approaches the lives cap of 300, and none of them is positive.

Shipman's 215 deaths score Lives −78 on the Overall line, a larger swing than Buffett's fine, from a smaller and grimmer number, because the unit is lives and the weight is 3×. The site is not saying a dollar and a death have been set equal. It is saying they are scored apart, and that only the death sits on the lives scale.

A harm that has been scored can only lower the Overall number. You can see it in the arithmetic the pages print. Buffett would be Jobs +88 without the −2. Dimon would be Jobs +87 without the −21. Cook would be Jobs +83 without the −23. The fine never pushes anyone up the list. If a frightening number is only in the unproven table, it does not push anyone down the list either. Both facts are visible on the same page, which is the point of leaving the unproven rows in place.
